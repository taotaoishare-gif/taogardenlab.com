Rosa's argument, as I understand it: modernity runs on acceleration, and the
compensation people reach for is control — more resources, more options, more
predictability. But the experiences that make a life feel worth living are
resonant ones, and resonance requires that the other party be genuinely able to
answer back on its own terms. He calls this *Unverfügbarkeit* — unavailability,
the quality of not being at one's disposal.

The consequence is uncomfortable and I think correct: **you cannot make
resonance happen.** A thing engineered to produce it is by definition available
to you, and availability is the property that forecloses it.

## Where this leaves me

This is the strongest argument against my own project that I have read, and it
comes from someone sympathetic to what I am trying to do.

I am building responsive environments. Responsive means: it answers when you
act. Available means: it is there when you want it. Those are close to the same
thing. A garden that reliably calms you is a garden at your disposal, and by
Rosa's account the reliability is what breaks it.

## Where I do not follow him

I think Rosa moves too quickly from "cannot be engineered" to a position that
is effectively hands-off, and I do not think the alternatives are only
*engineer it* or *leave it alone*.

Gardens are engineered. Rituals are engineered — extremely so, with rules about
where to stand and when to bow. Tea practice is engineered down to the size of
the cup. These are all *designed conditions under which something unavailable
may or may not occur*, and the tradition seems entirely comfortable with that
combination.

So the distinction I want is not designed versus undesigned. It is closer to:
does the system **guarantee** an outcome, or does it **make room** for one? A
guarantee makes the thing available. Room does not.

Whether that distinction survives contact with an actual build is unresolved.
The wobble mechanism in [Cosmic Pottery](/experiments/cosmic-pottery/) is a
place where I quietly crossed the line: the clay does not make room for
steadiness, it rewards it, and a reward is a guarantee with extra steps.

## What I will do with it

Two things. First, stop describing any piece as producing calm, presence, or
attention — those are outcomes and I should not be promising them. Second,
treat *Unverfügbarkeit* as a design constraint I can actually check against:
if a piece reliably delivers, that is evidence of a problem rather than
success.

I am aware that this is an unusually convenient reading, since it lets a
practice with no outcome data reframe the absence of outcomes as a virtue. I
want to note that I noticed.
