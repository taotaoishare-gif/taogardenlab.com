## 01 Observe

Between the road and the inside of the hall there are perhaps five separate
thresholds, and none of them stops anyone.

A wall with a gap in it. A raised step. A courtyard wide enough that you cross
it in the open. Another step, higher than a step needs to be. Then shoes off,
then a doorsill you have to lift your foot over.

Dogs asleep in the courtyard. It is not solemn. People are talking on their
phones right up until the sill.

Taking your shoes off is the moment something changes, and I think it is
because it is the only one that requires you to stop moving and use your
hands.

## 02 Contextualize

Lanna temple compounds (วัด) are walled precincts containing multiple
structures, typically raised on platforms, with a sequence of courtyards
between the outer wall and the assembly hall.

Removing footwear before entering is standard across Theravada Southeast Asia
and is not specific to Lanna, nor specific to temples — it applies to homes.
The raised sill (ธรณีประตู) has both practical and customary significance, and
stepping on rather than over it is avoided.

I should be clear that I have this from reading and from watching what other
people did, not from instruction.

## 03 Interpret

The problem being solved is **transition**. A person arriving from a street is
not in a state suitable for what happens in the hall, and no announcement can
change that.

The solution is a graded sequence of small physical demands, each of which
requires a tiny adjustment of the body: step up, cross open ground, step up
again, stop, bend, remove shoes, lift foot. None of them is symbolic in a way
you have to understand. They work on people who are not paying attention,
which is the population that most needs them.

Shoes are the strongest because they are irreversible on a short timescale.
Having removed them, leaving becomes effortful.

## 04 Translate

This is the entry with the most obvious application and the one I have used
least well.

Interfaces have no thresholds. An app opens instantly, which is treated as a
virtue. Every piece I have built has a veil or curtain at the start — a
deliberate hesitation before entry — and that came from here, but a veil you
click through in one second is a symbol of a threshold rather than a
threshold. There is no equivalent to bending down.

The untried version would ask for something physically irreversible-ish on a
short timescale. Putting a device down. Standing up.

## 05 Question

I am a visitor here with no standing in this tradition, and the risk of
turning a religious sequence into an interaction pattern is not hypothetical —
"mindfulness onboarding" already exists and it is largely this, stripped.

The specific thing I want to avoid: describing the shoe removal as an
"attention ritual" as though its function were psychological. Its function, to
the people doing it, is respect. That it also has an effect on the state of
the body is an observation I can make; treating the effect as the purpose
would be a misreading, and a self-serving one, since the effect is the part I
can use.
