## 01 Observe

The pot is small — smaller than a mug. Each infusion takes a few seconds and
produces about a mouthful each.

So the sequence repeats constantly: rinse, pour, offer, drink, refill. Nobody
announces it. The host does it while talking about something else, and the
conversation breaks every minute or so for the small business of the cups.

The breaks are what I noticed. A gap opens, someone hands you something, and
whatever was being said gets a pause in it. Difficult subjects seem to arrive
in the gaps.

Also: you cannot rush a drink that arrives in a mouthful.

## 02 Contextualize

工夫茶 in southern Fujian and Chaoshan uses a small pot or gaiwan, a high
leaf-to-water ratio, and many short infusions of the same leaves, tracking how
the flavour changes across them.

It is domestic and commercial rather than ceremonial — this is how business
gets done, and how visitors are received. It should not be assimilated to
Japanese chanoyu, which is a different practice with a different history and a
far more codified form.

## 03 Interpret

The human problem: **conversation has no natural punctuation.** Without it,
talk either runs continuously or stops awkwardly, and neither is comfortable
for saying difficult things.

Gongfu tea installs a metronome that no participant is responsible for. The
pause is attributable to the tea. Nobody has to say "let me think about that" —
the cups need refilling and the thinking happens in the gap.

This is a social technology disguised as a beverage.

## 04 Translate

The transferable principle is **externalised pauses**: a rhythm that comes
from the environment rather than from any participant, so that stopping
requires no one to take responsibility for stopping.

The incense timer in [Cosmic Pottery](/experiments/cosmic-pottery/) is the
closest I have come — a duration that nobody has to enforce. It works better
than a countdown for the same reason the cups work better than an agenda.

What I have not built is the *conversational* version, which is the more
interesting one and involves at least two people, which none of my pieces do.

## 05 Question

Whether the effect survives being made explicit. The pause works because the
tea is not a pause device — it is tea, and the rhythm is a side effect. Build
something whose stated purpose is to insert pauses into conversation and you
have a talking-stick app, which nobody wants, because the participants can now
see the mechanism operating on them.

That may be a general limit on this whole research programme: several of the
mechanisms I am collecting may only work while they are not perceived as
mechanisms. I do not know what to do with that, and I think it is more
important than most of the things I do know what to do with.
