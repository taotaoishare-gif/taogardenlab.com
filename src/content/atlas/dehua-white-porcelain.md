## 01 Observe

The workshops are loud. Radios, a compressor somewhere, people talking across
the room. Dust on everything, dust in the light.

The porcelain itself is the opposite of the room: warm-white, slightly
translucent at the edges, with almost no surface incident to look at. Held up,
the thin parts glow.

Greenware waiting to be fired is a different white — chalky, matte, fragile —
and the transformation in the kiln is more dramatic than I expected. Same
object, different substance.

Nobody in the workshop handles the pieces reverently. They are stock.

## 02 Contextualize

Dehua has produced white porcelain since at least the Song, and its Ming
output — known in Europe as *blanc de Chine* — was a significant export.
The local clay is unusually low in iron and high in potassium, which is why the
body can be fired to translucency and why the white has a warm rather than
blue cast.

Today it is a production centre: religious figures, export ceramics, and a
large volume of ordinary goods. The craft-heritage framing is real and it is
also a marketing category.

## 03 Interpret

What human problem does a material like this solve?

A surface with very little information gives attention nothing to consume. You
cannot scan it. Looking at Dehua white for any length of time, the looking
turns back on itself, because the object supplies almost no incident.

That is the same structural move as the lattice window in
[framing and borrowed scenery](/atlas/framing-borrowed-scenery/) — withhold
content, and something happens to the attention that was expecting it.

## 04 Translate

Materially: a screen cannot be translucent, and translucency is doing a lot of
the work. What can carry over is the **absence of incident** — surfaces with
no texture to inspect, no gradient to follow, no reward for scanning.

I built the celadon version of
[Cosmic Pottery](/experiments/cosmic-pottery/) partly out of this entry, and
the specific thing I took was restraint of surface detail rather than the
colour.

## 05 Question

The correction this entry makes to my own thinking is the one I most need to
keep.

I came to Dehua with an idea about porcelain and stillness. The stillness is a
property of the finished object in a quiet room — most often a room where
someone is selling it, or a museum. It is not a property of the practice,
which is noisy, repetitive, physical, and paid.

If I write about ceramics as a contemplative technology, I am describing a
consumer's relation to the object and calling it the maker's. Those are
different, and the second one is the one that carries the cultural
authority I would be borrowing.

There is a further question about who benefits. Extracting "the aesthetics of
slowness" from a production town, for an audience that will never buy from it,
transfers the prestige and none of the trade.
